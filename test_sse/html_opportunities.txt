<div class="section-label">03 — Causal Thesis</div>      <h2 class="section-title">Causal Thesis — Why Each Opportunity Exists, Why It Is Actionable</h2>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-USL-01 · Unsecured PIL margin scale-up — PRODUCT — Classification: HIGH POTENTIAL</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it exists: the 'Unsecured PIL' sub-product carries <strong>30.6% NIM</strong> (annualized) — roughly 3× the book average — yet holds only <strong>12.0%</strong> of USL balance (1,642.8 tỷ) and a mere <strong>0.36%</strong> penetration. At this NIM, the current balance already runs at an estimated <strong>502.2 tỷ/year</strong> annualized interest income; growing this balance compounds directly since the margin is structural, not promotional. Why actionable: it is a pricing/product mix already on TCB's book, not a new build. Why TCB is positioned: TCB already carries the highest unsecured lending cap (400M VND) and top TCB composite credit score in market for unsecured. What is in the plan: AP2027 WS1 (9 Sep 2026) names 'Unsecured lending acceleration' as a named cross-segment priority. Horizon: 6–12 months.</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-USL-02 · EMAF &amp; RMAS unsecured lending penetration gap — CUSTOMER SEGMENT — Classification: HIGH POTENTIAL</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it exists: EMAF (penetration 3.17%, +57.3% YoY holder growth) and RMAS (0.29%, +96.9% YoY) are TCB's fastest-growing USL customer counts but sit far below AFF's 9.81% and HNW's 11.7% penetration — both segments are also missing TOI budget by −24.0% and −40.8% respectively, i.e. the customers are being acquired but not deepened (limit utilization, cross-sell). Why actionable: acquisition is already working (the growth is happening); the gap is activation/limit usage, a servicing lever TCB controls directly. Why TCB is positioned: MAF/EMAF strategy already lists Embedded Finance (BNPL), progressive limit increase and U-point redemption for USL as planned 2026 CVP actions. What is in plan: AP2026 MAF/EMAF targets +89K PIL customers and +3,050 tỷ PIL balance uplift. Horizon: 6–9 months, tracks the existing plan cycle.</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-CCB-01 · Credit Card NIM/margin recapture — PRICING — Classification: HIGH POTENTIAL</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it exists: Card NIM is 0.66% vs a budgeted 1.47% — a shortfall of <strong>81 bps</strong> on a 26,590.62 tỷ balance, roughly <strong>215.4 tỷ</strong> of annualized interest income foregone. NFI (fee income) is +20.4% YoY and 103.2% of budget, masking this inside a near-on-budget TOI (91.9%). Why actionable: NII fell −66.3% YoY, meaning the interest-bearing (revolving) balance mix has shrunk faster than total balance (+4.6% YoY) — a repriceable/re-mixable driver, not a volume problem. Why TCB is positioned: TCB already prices five card tiers by segment (Visa Infinite 19.8% down to Visa Classic 38.8%); the lever is EMI/promo APR structure and revolve-encouraging offers, not new product build. Horizon: 3–6 months (next 1–2 pricing/campaign cycles).</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-CCB-02 · HNW credit card deepening — CUSTOMER SEGMENT — Classification: POTENTIAL</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it exists: HNW credit card penetration is already 60.99% (highest of all segments, +48.5% YoY holder growth) and TOI/customer is 4.28 mn actual vs 1.83 mn budget (+133.7%) — this segment is not under-penetrated, it is under-priced/under-cross-sold relative to its own demonstrated value. Why actionable: with acquisition largely done, the lever is spend/limit/product-tier upsell (Visa Infinite/Signature) on an existing base. Why TCB is positioned: TCB holds the top-tier card products already built for this segment. Contrary evidence: total HNW CCB TOI is only 58.27 tỷ (small in absolute terms) — a real but small pool. Horizon: 6 months.</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-USL-03 · HNW unsecured lending penetration — CUSTOMER SEGMENT — Classification: POTENTIAL</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it exists: HNW is USL's highest-yield segment (TOI/customer 17.19 mn actual vs 10.74 mn budget, +60.1%) yet penetration fell from 15.9% to 11.7% YoY — the HNW base is growing faster than USL adoption within it, and total HNW USL TOI missed budget by −19.4% (44.95 vs 55.75 tỷ). Why actionable: the profitability per customer is proven; the gap is coverage, not pricing. Contrary evidence: absolute pool is small (44.95 tỷ), capping near-term impact. Horizon: 6–9 months.</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-CCB-03 · MAF mass credit card base — CUSTOMER SEGMENT — Classification: WATCH</div>
<div class="insight-box">Why it may exist: MAF holds the single largest card base (528,770 holders, 17.84% penetration) but the slowest holder growth of any segment (+3.8% YoY) and the largest absolute TOI gap across both products (−81.93 tỷ vs budget). Why watch, not act yet: this segment is already covered by an existing plan lever (Spark Revamp card relaunch for MAF/EMAF, cited in TCB's segment strategy) and by PVcomBank's 4 new card lines launched 15 Sep 2026 targeting the same mass/general tier — the driver could be plan execution lag, competitive response, or both, and the brief could not separate them from the evidence at hand. Reconsideration trigger: re-open as POTENTIAL once Spark Revamp launch metrics (new cards, activation) are available for at least one full campaign cycle.</div>
<div class="section-subtitle" style="margin-top:28px">OPP-USL-04 · Staff overdraft preferential margin — PRODUCT — Classification: NO ACTIONABLE OPPORTUNITY</div>
<div class="insight-box">OD Staff carries 1,707.60 tỷ balance (12.45% of USL) at only 2.29% NIM — the lowest margin sub-product — but this reflects the staff preferential-rate HR policy (LSCS CBNV spread), not a market or execution gap. There is no commercial lever to close this margin without changing staff benefits policy, which sits outside RBG Retail's remit. Closed: no further action tracked under this brief.</div>
